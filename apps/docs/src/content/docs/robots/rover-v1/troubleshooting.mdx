Something's off? Find the module that's misbehaving below — each section lists
the symptoms we see most and the fix that almost always works.

## ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 dev board (N8R2)

### Computer doesn't see the board over USB

Use a USB-C cable that carries data (many only charge), and plug into the port labelled USB, not UART.

### Upload fails or times out

Hold the BOOT button while plugging in the cable, then try the upload again.

## 1S Li-ion power board (charger + 5V boost)

### Robot won't turn on

Check the 18650 is inserted with + facing the mark, the slide switch is on, and the cell has charge — plug in USB-C and look for the charging light.

### Robot dies the moment the motors start

The cell is nearly empty or worn out — charge it fully, or try a known-good branded protected cell.

## DRV8833 dual motor driver

### One wheel spins the wrong way

Swap that motor's two wires at the driver, or flip its +/- in the builder's motor settings.

### Motors twitch or robot reboots when driving

Battery is low or wires too thin — charge the cell and check the 5V/GND wires are seated.

## N20 metal-gear motor (6V 150RPM)

### One wheel never spins

Check both motor wires are firmly seated at the driver board and that the thin solder tabs on the motor haven't snapped off.

### Motor whirrs but the wheel barely moves

Push the wheel further onto the D-shaped shaft until it seats — it should not slip when you twist it by hand.

## VL53L0X time-of-flight distance sensor

### Distance always reads 0 or a huge number

SDA and SCL are probably swapped — swap the blue and yellow wires at the brain.

### Readings are jumpy or too short

Peel the tiny protective film off the sensor window and keep wires out of its view.

## 2-channel TCRT5000 line sensor

### Robot ignores the line

Keep the sensors 2-5 mm above the floor and use dark tape on a light surface — faint or glossy lines won't register.

### Readings jump around

Bright sunlight and halogen lamps blind infrared sensors — try a shadier spot.

## 2-pixel WS2812 LED eyes

### Eyes stay dark

Data must enter at the pin marked DIN (the arrows point away from it) — if the wire is on DOUT, move it to the other end.

### Colours look wrong

Some boards use a different colour order — swap the red/green channel order in the builder's LED settings.

## Passive piezo buzzer

### No sound at all

Check the + leg (the longer one, marked on top) goes to the yellow signal wire, and that yours is a PASSIVE buzzer — active ones only play one fixed tone.

### Sound is very quiet

Seat the buzzer fully in its pocket and make sure the small hole on top isn't covered.

## SPDT slide switch

### Switch does nothing

Solder to the MIDDLE pin plus one outer pin — the two outer pins are never connected to each other.

### Robot flickers when you touch the switch

A solder joint is loose — reheat both joints until the solder flows shiny and smooth.

## General

### Robot won't turn on

Check the power switch is in the on position, then check the cell: charge it
fully and make sure it is seated the right way around. If the robot runs on
USB but not on battery, the cell (or its protection circuit) is the culprit.

### Can't find the robot on the network

Your robot and your phone/laptop must be on the **same Wi-Fi network**. If
they are and it still doesn't appear, re-provision: hold **BOOT** for 5
seconds to factory-reset Wi-Fi, then run the [first-run setup](./first-run/)
again. On iOS, prefer the robot's IP address over the `.local` name.

Still stuck? Take a photo of your wiring next to the
[wiring diagram](./wiring/) — nine times out of ten the answer is in that
comparison.
